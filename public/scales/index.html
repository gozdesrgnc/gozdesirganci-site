Scale development · Adaptation · Validity

Can we trust what this scale measures?

A questionnaire score is only useful if the items measure the construct they claim to measure, measure it consistently, and work the same way for the people who answer them. Scale development builds a new instrument from a clear definition of the construct. Scale adaptation carries an existing instrument into a new language or culture, and then has to show again that it still works.

I work with researchers in health, sport and education who need an instrument that will hold up in review: planning the study, choosing the analyses, and building the reliability and validity evidence that goes into the paper.

From construct to validated scale

The development sequence

  1. Define the construct

    Literature review and a written definition of what the scale should and should not cover.

  2. Write the item pool

    More items than needed, with a clear response format and wording rules.

  3. Expert review

    Content validity ratings from subject experts, then revision of weak items.

  4. Pilot study

    A first sample to check item statistics, clarity and response patterns.

  5. Factor structure

    Exploratory and confirmatory factor analysis to test the intended dimensions.

  6. Reliability

    Internal consistency, composite reliability and, where needed, test–retest.

  7. Measurement invariance

    Checking that the scale works the same way across gender, age or other groups.

  8. Report and interpret

    Scoring guidance and a write-up that reviewers and users can follow.

For adaptations, steps one and two become forward and back translation, expert review of cultural fit and language equivalence, and a check that the original factor structure holds in the new sample.
