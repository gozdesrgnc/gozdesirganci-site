Fairness · DIF · Measurement invariance

Does this test measure everyone the same way?

A test is fair when two people with the same level of the skill have the same chance of answering an item correctly, whatever group they belong to. When that fails for a specific item, psychometricians call it differential item functioning (DIF). When it fails for a whole scale, so that scores cannot be compared across countries, languages or genders, we say the scale lacks measurement invariance.

DIF is different from a group simply scoring lower. Groups can differ in ability. DIF means the item itself behaves differently once ability is held constant, often because of wording, cultural context or content that one group has seen more than the other.

The questions my research asks

Research lines

Which items are unfair, and how do we find them?

Comparing DIF detection methods for dichotomous and polytomous items, from logistic regression and Poly-SIBTEST to lasso-penalized IRT, machine learning and generative AI.

Can scores be compared across many groups?

Testing measurement invariance in large-scale assessments such as PISA with alignment optimization and Bayesian approximate invariance, where dozens of countries are compared at once.

Where does DIF come from?

Explaining DIF with mixture Rasch and IRT-MIMIC models, covariates and latent classes, and asking whether DIF changes across ability levels.
